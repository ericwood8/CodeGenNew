# API_Subscriptions_v1 and the SubscriptionTable, PlanTable, PlanLimitTable, PlanLimits, SubscriptionActiveStatuses, SubscriptionRequired and SubscriptionWebhookSecret settings

Plans, subscriptions and entitlements for a SaaS API, on top of the tenants (`TenantTable`, `API_Tenants_v1.md`). A tenant is on a plan; its subscription has a status; the plan says how much of something the tenant may have. The payment gateway is outside the generator: the generated code holds only the ids and the status the gateway reports, and no card data is ever a column. The rule is `SubscriptionPlan` in Core, the text `SubscriptionWriter`.

```
TenantTable=Tenant
TenantMembership=EmployeeTenant:EmployeeId:TenantId:Role
SubscriptionTable=Subscription:PlanId:Status:CurrentPeriodEnd:GatewaySubscriptionId
PlanTable=Plan
PlanLimitTable=PlanLimit:PlanId:LimitKey:LimitValue
PlanLimits=Project:projects,EmployeeTenant:seats
SubscriptionActiveStatuses=trialing,active
SubscriptionRequired=false
SubscriptionWebhookSecret=GATEWAY_WEBHOOK_SECRET
```

It needs `ApiAuth`, `AccessMode=Ef` and `TenantTable`. Every part after the table name is optional and found by its usual name.

## The three tables

| Setting | The table |
|---|---|
| `SubscriptionTable` | Tenant-owned (a NOT NULL `TenantId` that points at the tenant table), a single whole-number key, a NOT NULL whole-number plan column (default `PlanId`), a NOT NULL text status column (`Status`), optionally a period end (`CurrentPeriodEnd`, `PeriodEnd`, `TrialEndsOn` or `EndsOn`; date and time), a gateway id (`GatewaySubscriptionId`, `GatewayId` or `ExternalId`; text) and a period start (`CurrentPeriodStart` or `PeriodStart`; stamped when the row is made). Every other column must allow NULL or have a default, since the platform route makes the row. One row per tenant counts: the newest (largest key) when there are several. |
| `PlanTable` | The plan the subscription points at. A single whole-number key; its `Name` or `Title` is shown, a number column named `Price`, `MonthlyPrice`, `PriceMonthly` or `Amount` is its price and a text column named `BillingPeriod`, `BillingInterval`, `Period` or `Interval` its billing period (both shown beside the plan, and in the list of plans). Any other column is yours. |
| `PlanLimitTable` | A row per plan and limit: the plan (a foreign key to the plan table), a text limit name (`LimitKey`, `Key`, `Kind` or `Name`) and a whole number (`LimitValue`, `Value`, `MaxValue`, `Limit` or `Quantity`). NULL, or no row for a limit, means unlimited. |

These three tables get **no ordinary list and edit API** (`ProjectSettings.IsSubscriptionManaged`, the same way the side table of a row approval has none): such a screen would let a tenant's administrator change a plan, a limit or their own subscription. The routes below are the only way to read or change them. Plans and limits are data you maintain with SQL or your own back office.

## What is generated (`Apis/Subscriptions.cs`)

- **Writes pause while the subscription is not active.** A middleware (`SubscriptionCheck`, mapped by `SubscriptionRoutes`) answers 402 (`/problems/subscription-inactive`) to a POST, PUT, PATCH or DELETE from a caller who works in a tenant whose status is not in `SubscriptionActiveStatuses` (default `trialing,active`, compared without regard to case). A `trialing` subscription also stops at its period end. Reads are never paused; neither are sign-in and the tenant switch (`/api/auth/...` except the members routes) nor the subscription routes. A caller in no tenant (a platform administrator) is not checked. A tenant with no subscription row is on no plan and is not limited, unless `SubscriptionRequired=true` (then 402). The tenant's state is remembered ten seconds, and forgotten at once when the subscription is changed through the routes below.
- **Plan limits.** `PlanLimits=Project:projects,EmployeeTenant:seats` (a table and the limit's name): a create in a limited table is refused with 402 (`/problems/plan-limit`, "The plan allows 5 for projects and this tenant already has 5.") when the tenant already has what its plan allows. The create, its clone (`{id}/clone`) and its CSV import (`import`) are guarded; for the `TenantMembership` table the guard is `POST /api/auth/members`, so a limit named `seats` stops adding people past the plan. The count is the tenant's rows of the table, not counting a soft-deleted row (`SoftDeleteTables`). The limit is read at the create, so a raised limit works at once. A limited table needs an API of its own (or be the membership table).
- **`GET /api/subscription`** (the administrator of the tenant): `{ hasSubscription, planId, planName, planPrice, planPeriod, status, periodEnd, gatewayId, writable, reason, limits: [{ key, allowed, used }], plans: [{ planId, name, price, period, current, limits: [{ key, allowed }] }] }` for the tenant in the token. `allowed` is null for an unlimited limit; `plans` lists every plan, cheapest first (a plan with no price last), with what it allows of each limit and `current` on the tenant's own, so a page can show what a larger plan would give. A caller who is not an administrator gets 403; a platform administrator, who works in no tenant, gets the no-tenant problem.
- **`PUT /api/subscription/{tenantId}`** (a platform administrator only: the administrator role, working in no tenant): `{ planId?, status?, periodEnd?, gatewayId? }` changes the subscription of that tenant and answers the same view. A tenant with no subscription needs a `planId` and a `status` and gets a new row (400 otherwise); an unknown tenant is 404 and an unknown plan 400. The route is how a back office, or your payment gateway's webhook handler through your own code, sets the status.
- **`POST /api/subscription/webhook`** (only with a gateway id column on the subscription table and `SubscriptionWebhookSecret`, the name of the environment variable that holds the shared secret; the secret itself is never part of the project): the payment gateway tells the API a subscription's new status. Nobody signs in for it; the signature is the check. The body is `{ id?, gatewayId, status, periodEnd? }` and the header `X-Gateway-Signature: t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex>` carries the HMAC-SHA256 (hex) of `<t>.<body>` with the secret. A message with no secret configured is 503, a missing or wrong signature 401 (`/problems/subscription-webhook-signature`), a signed time more than five minutes from the server's 400 (`/problems/subscription-webhook-expired`, a replay), a body with no `gatewayId` or `status` 400, an unknown gateway id 404, and an event `id` seen in the last ten minutes answers 200 `{ applied: false, duplicate: true }` without applying it again. Otherwise it sets the status (and period end), forgets the remembered state and answers 200 `{ applied: true }`. Put an adapter in front of it when the gateway signs its messages another way. The event ids are remembered in memory, so a second API process does not know them.
- **`SubscriptionGateway.ApplyAsync(services, gatewayId, status, periodEnd)`** (ASP.NET, with a gateway id column): what the webhook calls; it finds the subscription by the gateway's id, sets the status (and period end), forgets the remembered state and answers false when no subscription has that id.
- **`SubscriptionTestSupport`**: used by the generated tests (a plan from a JSON body, the limits, the removal of what a test made).

## Generated tests (`API_TestSubscriptions`, with `ApiTests`)

`SubscriptionApiTests.cs` makes a tenant and a plan of its own with a platform token for each test and removes them again: writes pause while the subscription is cancelled and go on when it is active again, reads are never paused; a tenant with no subscription writes (or does not, with `SubscriptionRequired`); a create past the plan limit is refused until the limit is raised, and the view shows `allowed` and `used`; a trial ends at its period end; only a platform administrator changes a subscription (an administrator inside a tenant is 403, an unknown tenant 404, an incomplete or unknown-plan body 400); the administrator sees the subscription and a member does not. The write tests use the first limited table with an API whose only foreign key is the tenant, else the first such tenant-owned table.

`The_view_lists_every_plan_with_what_it_allows_and_marks_the_tenants_own` checks the plan list, and with the webhook `The_webhook_sets_the_status_of_the_subscription_the_gateway_names_and_refuses_what_is_not_signed` signs its own messages with the secret in the environment variable (the test is inconclusive when the variable is not set): no signature, a wrong one, an hour-old message, the status set, writes paused at once, an event applied once, an unknown gateway id and an incomplete message.

The generated tests of the other tables work in the first active tenant, so that tenant's plan needs room for the rows they add (or it has no subscription). `Docs/Verification/Tools/subscription_check.py` plays the same rules, and the seats limit through `POST /api/auth/members`, against a running API over `Schemas/subscriptions.sql`.

## The other stacks

- **Python and Rust APIs** (`PY_Subscriptions`, `RS_Subscriptions`; `app/routes/subscriptions.py`, `src/subscriptions.rs`; text in Core `PythonSubscriptionWriter` and `RustSubscriptionWriter`): the same pause (402), plan limits, `GET /api/subscription` (with the price and the list of plans), `PUT /api/subscription/{tenant_id}` and the webhook, with the same sentences. `require_user` calls `check_write` for a caller who works in a tenant; the webhook sits in a router that needs no sign-in (`public_router`). The Rust API remembers a tenant's subscription for the seconds in `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_SECONDS` (default 10; the tests set 0, since they run side by side in one process). A period end may be an RFC 3339 time with an offset, a date and time (taken as UTC) or a date.
- **Their generated tests** (`PY_TestSubscriptions` writes `tests/test_subscriptions.py`, `RS_TestSubscriptions` writes `tests/api/subscription_routes.rs`; `PythonTests=true` or `RustTests=true` with `SubscriptionTable`; not for SQL Server): the same cases as the ASP.NET tests, with a tenant made through the API (`tenant_body` of `PY_TestTenants`, `make_tenant` of `RS_TestTenants`) and a plan made with SQL under a fixed key (`SubscriptionTestSql`). The Python tests sign in with real tokens, since the check lives in `require_user`; the webhook tests set their own secret. `Docs/Verification/Tools/subscription_check.py` plays the same rules against a running API of any of the three.
- **Subscription page** (`TSX_Subscription`, `TS_Subscription`, `BLZ_Subscription`, `UNO_Subscription`, `WinUI3_Subscription`): with `Auth` and a sign-in, the menu shows administrators a Subscription page with the plan and its price, the status, the period end, whether changes are allowed (or why they are paused), a table of the limits with what the plan allows (Unlimited when none) and what the tenant has, and a table of every plan with its price and what it allows (the tenant's own marked). The React, Angular and Blazor pages have generated tests.
- **The message in the add form:** a save refused with 402 shows the API's sentence instead of "Problem while saving!": in React (`planDetail` in a tenant-owned table's page), Angular (the notifier already shows the problem's detail), Blazor and Uno (`ApiSupport` reads the problem detail into `ApiException`, and `Explain` returns it). The Add member dialog of the members page shows the seats sentence the same way in all four.
- **WinUI3** reads the database itself and never meets the API's check, so `SubscriptionGuard` (`Repositories/SubscriptionGuard.cs`) is the same rule for the app's own context: the detail and master-detail dialogs call `SubscriptionGuard.RefusalAsync` before they save (a paused subscription, or a new row past the limit, is the dialog's error), the Add member dialog checks the seats limit, and the Subscription page reads the same data. It is a check in the client: anyone who writes to the database directly bypasses it. The list screen's delete, restore and clone, the child grid's delete and the child rows saved with a master-detail dialog (a new row counts against the limit) are checked too. Not checked: the link and unlink of a junction table, the screens of a single row, a queue retry and the approval buttons. `WinUI3_TestSubscriptions` writes `SubscriptionGuardTests.cs` (with `DesktopTests=true`): the guard over an in-memory SQLite database made from the generated context, with a tenant, a plan, its limit and a subscription (a paused subscription, a new row past the limit until it is raised, a trial that ends, no subscription, a caller in no tenant).

## Not covered

Choosing a plan from the list (the page shows the plans; a platform administrator or your own back office moves the tenant), the payment gateway itself and a signature scheme other than the one above, a status held in a look-up table, usage metering (item 12) and feature flags a plan switches on (item 14). The master-detail pages of React, Angular and Blazor show the general failure for a refused save, not the sentence. A CSV import counts as one create, so one import can pass a limit. Two creates at the same moment can both pass the limit (the count and the insert are two steps).
